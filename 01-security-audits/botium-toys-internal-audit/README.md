# Botium Toys – Internal Security Audit

## Overview

This project documents an internal security audit performed for
Botium Toys, a fictional U.S.-based toy company.

The objective of the audit was to assess the company's existing
security controls and compliance posture and identify areas that
could introduce security or regulatory risk.

## Scenario

Botium Toys operates a physical store, warehouse, internal IT
infrastructure, e-commerce systems and processes customer data,
including payment-related information and PII/SPII.

The audit covers the organization's security program, assets,
controls, internal processes and compliance practices.

## Frameworks / Standards

- NIST Cybersecurity Framework (NIST CSF)
- PCI DSS
- GDPR
- SOC 1 / SOC 2

## Audit Scope

The audit assessed:

- IT assets
- Internal network
- Data storage and retention
- Employee devices
- Security controls
- Access controls
- Physical security
- Compliance practices

## Key Findings

The assessment identified several security gaps, including:

- Lack of least-privilege access controls
- Lack of separation of duties
- Lack of encryption for customer credit-card information
- No intrusion detection system
- No disaster recovery plan
- No backups of critical data
- Weak password policy
- No centralized password management system
- Inadequate asset management and classification

## Risk Assessment

Overall risk score: 8/10

The organization presents a relatively high level of risk due to
missing security controls and gaps in compliance best practices.

## Compliance Areas

### PCI DSS

The audit assessed controls related to:

- Access to cardholder data
- Secure handling of payment information
- Encryption
- Password management

### GDPR

The audit assessed:

- Protection of EU customer data
- Breach notification procedures
- Data classification and inventory
- Privacy policies and procedures

### SOC

The audit assessed:

- User access policies
- PII/SPII confidentiality
- Data integrity
- Data availability

## Recommendations

Recommendations were developed based on the identified control
and compliance gaps.

Priority should be given to controls that reduce the risk of
unauthorized access, data exposure, loss of critical data and
regulatory non-compliance.
